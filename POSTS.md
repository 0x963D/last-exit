## LinkedIn — main post

The border crossing at the start of Cyberpunk stuck with me. Sitting across from a guard, trying to make your story hold together.

So I built a small version where TypeSafe's Jev decides what the inspector does next.

You write the cover story. He can check your papers, scan the car, or let you through.

It's called Last Exit. See if you can get past him:

## LinkedIn — first comment

I ran 100 scripted crossings before sharing it: 50 authored variants, each repeated twice. Smugglers escaped 29/90 times; honest shipments passed 10/10. The batch used 596 Jev requests and approximately $0.057 in estimated input-token cost.

Median reply time on the local API was 537 ms for two sequential calls, excluding browser paint. Public hosting adds overhead. This isn't a general model benchmark: the scenario and action rules matter, and it still misreads some explanations.

Jev reads the player's statements and selects actions. The spoken lines and physical consequences are authored code. The guard never sees the hidden cargo flag.

Source, full methodology and fictional transcripts: https://github.com/0x963D/last-exit
